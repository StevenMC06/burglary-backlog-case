# Pilot, trust, and operational feasibility

## Slide-ready recommendation
Launch evidence navigation, not predictive patrol or automated suspect identification. AI flags candidate timestamps; humans inspect the original footage and decide whether anything is relevant. A match is a lead, never a finding of guilt. Do not assert the system recognizes intent from video.

## 90-day pilot (proposed design, not an existing result)
- Days 1–15: co-design with two patrol officers, two detectives, records/evidence staff, security, prosecutor liaison, and a community/privacy representative. Define eligible cases and lawful access boundaries. Record the existing workflow. Training: one 90-minute practical session plus a 30-minute calibration exercise. These staffing/training numbers are planning assumptions and must be costed.
- Days 16–30: replay 100 synthetic/public-video packages with known event labels. Separate calibration and held-out evaluation sets. Each package has evidence-relevant events, ordinary activity, night/day scenes, camera movement, low resolution, and occlusion. Include obvious failure cases. Two human annotators reconcile ground truth before scoring. Public availability is not permission to identify real people; stage synthetic people/objects for the demo.
- Days 31–75: proposed future agency pilot, only after data/security approval: 300 eligible new burglary cases randomized 1:1 to usual review or AI-assisted review. Stratify by footage duration/quality and investigator team. If likely linked incidents share evidence, randomize the incident cluster and account for clustering. Log all assigned cases, including non-use and failures; compare intention-to-treat. Ground-truth reviewers inspect full source footage independent of AI output, blinded to arm where practical. Budget evaluation/audit time separately; do not bury it in time-saving estimates. The competition itself continues to use only public/synthetic data.
- Days 76–90: compare labor, quality, usability, and costs; inspect misses; independently audit. Publish aggregate findings and limitations. Expand only if the predeclared efficiency threshold is met and safety gates pass; otherwise revise or stop. A 300-case pilot is a proposed feasibility sample, not a power guarantee.

## One primary efficiency metric
Mean total human evidence-review minutes per randomized eligible case, including upload preparation, verification, corrections, fallback review, and handoff. Pre-register the target from the financial model and report mean difference with confidence intervals, workload distribution, and adoption. Track time-to-first-verified-lead separately; faster elapsed time is not equal to fewer labor hours.

## Safety and trust gates (proposed acceptance policy)
- Never delete or hide source footage. Every accepted flag links to immutable source ID/hash, camera/time metadata, original timestamp, model/version, prompt/configuration, and reviewer acceptance/rejection. Hashing proves integrity, not truth or legal admissibility.
- Record evidence-event recall (missed-event rate) and precision (false positives) on full-footage ground truth. Review subgroup/camera-condition results; camera availability and quality create unequal coverage. Safety margin must be set with evidence users before looking at pilot results; do not invent a universal '95% accurate' threshold.
- No arrest, stop, search, suspect identity, or case clearance based solely on AI. Human reviews original evidence and applicable authority; AI output is separated from source evidence in the packet. Supervisor review for any operational escalation.
- No facial recognition, protected-trait inference, neighborhood risk scores, or patrol allocation. Give non-video cases normal access to investigation; do not make camera ownership the gate for service. Audit access and triage outcomes across areas without feeding historical police activity into deployment decisions.
- Access controls, encryption/key responsibility, audit records, retention/deletion policy, supplier access, incident response, and applicable personnel controls require agency approval. Public chatbots and team Discord/GitHub are never operational case-data stores.

## By year one
Stage rollout contingent on observed savings. Maintain a contemporaneous control or phased randomized rollout. Primary effectiveness outcome: proportion of eligible burglary incidents cleared within a fixed follow-up window (e.g., 180 days), measured identically in both arms. Report citywide clearance separately, because only some cases are eligible and the case target is citywide. Show denominators, arrest vs exceptional clearance, prior-year backlog, missing follow-up, and confidence intervals; do not treat an administratively closed record or property recovery as a clearance. Track substantiated complaints and correction/reversal rates.

The trial must use the eligible-case baseline, not automatically the citywide 13%. Achieving 325 additional citywide clearances from 6,800 treated cases requires a 4.78 percentage-point incremental yield per treated case. At 80% eligibility, the eligible assignment-level effect needed is 325/8,000 = 4.06 percentage points before trial-specific non-use patterns. Adoption must not be multiplied twice. Commission a study-specific power calculation accounting for clustering, eligibility, attrition and follow-up. A 300-case pilot establishes feasibility and efficiency, not the citywide clearance claim. Late-year enrollments cannot supply mature 180-day outcomes by year end.

Evaluation budget correction: adding one extra full-source audit pass for 300 packages at an assumed 120 minutes and $50/hour requires $30,000, bringing the one-time allowance to $180,000. Footage duration is an assumption; more reviewers or longer footage increase that cost.
