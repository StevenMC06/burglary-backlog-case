# Team 6 recommendation and presentation data

## Decision
Approve a staged, source-linked video-review pilot. AI flags candidate timestamps; officers inspect original footage; detectives receive an approved evidence packet. Preserve all original footage, including events the AI misses. No facial recognition, suspect identity decisions, or predictive patrol allocation.

This is a researched proposal with scenario math, not a validated deployment. The two competition prototypes are available as synthetic fixture workflows, not live AI inference. No production performance has been measured.

## Three delegated research streams
1. Solution and evidence: video review versus detective linkage, verified research, limits of causal claims.
2. Economics: repeatable conservative/base/optimistic model, funded follow-up, first-year ramp, sensitivity, ROI and payback.
3. Pilot and trust: evaluation, CJIS requirements, source traceability, false positives/misses, and human authority.

## Case targets
Fictional baseline: 10,000 burglaries/year; 13% clearance; $3.05M annual investigative labor. Clearance target is 16.25%, requiring 325 additional clearances. Net cost target is $457,500/year.

All are assumptions, not observed effects or vendor quotes. None meets the 15% savings target. Monetized staff capacity does not become cash savings unless finance identifies avoidable expenditure.

Base: 10,000 ×80% eligible ×85% adoption ×60 net saved officer minutes /60 ×$50 = $340,000 gross capacity. Reserve20% ($68,000 /1,360 officer hours) for extra evidence work. Subtract$165,500 recurring costs including$91,000 new detective follow-up. Net=$106,500. One-time setup$180,000. With65% effective year-one ramp:272,000×.65−165,500−180,000=−168,700. Economic ROI−48.83%; illustrative payback2.58years, contingent on realizing labor value and subsequent full adoption.

## Clearance mechanism and workload
1,000 genuinely new, deduplicated lead-bearing cases ×65% completed follow-up ×50% incremental conversion =325 additional clearances. Every input is unproven. A controlled evaluation must determine incrementality. Half as many leads, or half the conversion, yields about163 additional cases and14.63% total clearance.

650 follow-ups ×2 detective hours ×$70 =$91,000, included above. This uncertain allowance must cover all additional investigation through disposition. Four hours doubles this cost and lowers base net to$15,500. Extra triage for incomplete follow-ups also needs funding if outside existing allowances. Do not count reserved officer time as savings.

## Pilot
First30days: co-design and training, synthetic ground-truth tests. Proposed future agency pilot:300 eligible cases randomized1:1, after security and lawful-access approval. Primary efficiency metric: total human review minutes including preparation, checks, corrections, fallback, and handoff. Predeclare a60-minute base savings hypothesis and evidence-quality safety gates. Keep non-users and failed cases in the analysis.

By day90: independent full-footage audit of missed evidence, false positives, adoption and costs. Expand only on measured safe efficiency. By year one: controlled rollout and results for cohorts with mature180-day outcomes, citywide and eligible denominators separately. Late-year cases require follow-up beyond year one. A300-case pilot is not sufficient proof of13%→16.25% clearance uplift. Power must be planned for the eligible-case baseline, adoption and clustering; see independent-review.md.
