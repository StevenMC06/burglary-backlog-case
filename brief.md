# Case brief

Source: supplied competition DOCX, Cracking the Burglary Backlog. All department baseline figures are fictional.

## Decision
Recommend a focused AI intervention for the Chief and Council. Show a credible effect on clearance, labor costs, adoption, and trust.

## Baseline
10,000 cases/year; 13% clearance (1,300 cases); officer $50/hour; detective $70/hour.

| Task | Minutes/case | Role | Annual labor value |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Intake | 15 | Officer | $125,000 |
| Report writing | 60 | Officer | $500,000 |
| Video review | 120 | Officer | $1,000,000 |
| Evidence processing | 45 | Officer | $375,000 |
| Detective analysis | 90 | Detective | $1,050,000 |

Total: 330 minutes/case, $305/case, $3.05M/year.
Video review is largest by time; detective analysis is largest by labor value.

## Targets
- 25% relative clearance increase: 13% × 1.25 = 16.25%, or 325 additional clearances/year at fixed volume. About 16% is the rounded case target.
- 15% net cost reduction: $457,500/year after solution costs.
- Targets are asks, not pass/fail thresholds. Honest shortfalls are acceptable.

## Constraints and deliverables
No facial recognition; only public/synthetic competition data; human decisions; a future operational deployment must meet CJIS requirements. Minimize extra officer work. Measure actual time including review and corrections.
Under 10 slides per organizer email. Cover bottleneck, workflow, economics, clearance mechanism, 90-day/year-one pilot, ethics, and ideally a working prototype with a 60–90 second recording.

## Open questions
- Which bottleneck has both measurable time savings and a credible clearance mechanism?
- What fraction of cases is eligible and actually uses the tool?
- Which benefits are cash savings versus redeployed capacity?
- How do we measure missed evidence, false positives, and clearance without overclaiming?
